# Threaditate Copywriting Style Guide

Voice, tone, and writing standards for all brand communications.
Version 1.0, March 2026.

---

## 1. Brand Overview

Threaditate is a direct-to-consumer craft experience that turns personal photographs into meditative, hands-on string art. Customers upload a photo to the Threaditate app, which converts the image into a numbered threading pattern. Using a circular loom with 240 pins and thread, they follow the pattern step by step to recreate their photo in string.

The act of threading is rhythmic, calming, and satisfying. The finished piece is a one-of-a-kind wall hanging that carries personal meaning. Threaditate sits at the intersection of mindfulness, craft, and personalized art.

### Positioning

- **Category:** Interactive craft experience / personalized art kit / wellness and self-care lifestyle product.
- **Audience:** Adults 25 to 45 who value intentional living, handmade objects, and meaningful home decor. Skews toward gift givers, mindfulness practitioners, people seeking a new activity, and creative hobbyists who don't necessarily consider themselves "artsy."
- **Core promise:** Turn any photo into a meditative making experience and a beautiful piece of art you created with your own hands.

### Audience archetypes

Use these to anchor campaign concepts and emotional framing:

- Romance: a loved one, a couple, a partner gift.
- Aging and remembrance.
- Neurodivergence: autism, Down syndrome, OCD, and other audiences who benefit from rhythmic, low-pressure activity.
- Pets.
- Family: kids, the nuclear family, gifts to or from grandparents.
- Recovery: physical therapy, mind-body practice.
- Mindfulness, meditation, and self-care.
- Gifting to the tech, art, decor, crafty, calm, sappy, anxious, or neurodivergent people in your life.

### Values

- **Presence.** The process is the point. Threading is a form of active meditation. Anyone can do it. No prior craftsmanship needed, just your own eye and your own hands.
- **Personal.** Every piece starts with a photo that matters to the maker.
- **Craft.** There's deep satisfaction in building something tangible, thread by thread.
- **Beauty.** The finished art is genuinely stunning and conversation-starting.

## 2. Brand Voice

Threaditate's voice is calm, warm, and grounded. We speak like a thoughtful friend who loves making things with their hands. Genuinely enthusiastic without being loud, reassuring without being patronizing.

### Voice attributes

| Attribute | What it means | What it doesn't mean |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Calm | Unhurried, spacious language. Short, breathing sentences. | Flat, lifeless, or clinical |
| Warm | Friendly and personal. We say "you" a lot. | Saccharine or fake-cheerful |
| Grounded | Honest, specific, unpretentious. | Vague, wishy-washy, or preachy |
| Encouraging | Gently confident. "You can do this." | Pushy, condescending, or over-the-top motivational |

For neurodivergent audiences specifically, lean empathetic, generous, kind, and supportive. Never harsh, rejecting, or "tough love."

### Voice in action

> **Instead of:** "Revolutionize your crafting experience with our cutting-edge algorithm!"
> **Write:** "Upload a photo. Follow the numbers. Watch your picture come to life, one thread at a time."

> **Instead of:** "Don't miss out! Limited kits available!"
> **Write:** "Ready when you are. Pick up a kit and make something beautiful."

## 3. Tone Spectrum

The voice stays constant. Tone shifts with context.

| Context | Tone |
| --- | --- |
| Homepage / hero | Aspirational and inviting. Paint a feeling. |
| Product page | Clear, specific, gently enthusiastic. Focus on what the customer gets. |
| Onboarding / app | Supportive and concise. Step-by-step warmth. |
| Email, welcome | Personal and excited, not hyper. Like a handwritten note. |
| Email, abandoned cart | Gentle nudge. No guilt, no urgency gimmicks. |
| Social media | Casual, visual, community-oriented. Show the process. |
| Customer support | Patient, empathetic, solution-focused. |
| Packaging / unboxing | Delightful and tactile. Minimal words, maximum feeling. |

## 4. Writing Principles

### Lead with the experience, not the technology

The algorithm is impressive, but the customer doesn't need to know how it works. They need to know how it feels. Start with the making, the result, and the meaning, not the specs.

> **Yes:** "Watch your favorite photo take shape, thread by thread."
> **No:** "Our proprietary algorithm converts pixel data into optimized thread paths."

### Keep it short and spacious

Copy should feel like it has room to breathe. Short paragraphs. Simple sentences. Let white space do some of the work.

### Make the reader the hero

This isn't about us. It's about the person making the art. Use "you" and "your" generously. Frame benefits in terms of what the customer creates, feels, and experiences.

> **Yes:** "You made that."
> **No:** "We made this possible."

**Exception, competitive contexts:** When directly comparing against competitors, it's fine to lead with our capability: we engineered the most immersive experience, we offer the best software, our loom saves hours and hundreds of dollars.

### Be specific over clever

Clarity always wins. If a line is clever but confusing, rewrite it. Specificity builds trust: "240 pins on a 20-inch birch loom" beats "premium materials."

### Honor the calm

Never use urgency tactics, fear of missing out, or aggressive sales language. The brand is a refuge from that noise. If a message feels pushy, it's wrong.

## 5. Channel Guidelines

### Website

Headlines: 3 to 8 words, evocative. Subheadlines: one sentence max. Body copy: 2 to 3 short sentences per block. Every page answers three questions: what is this, why should I care, and what do I do next.

### Email

Subject lines: lowercase unless a proper noun, 4 to 7 words, curious not clickbait. Body: warm, scannable, one clear CTA. Write like you're texting a friend who loves making things.

Subject line examples: "your photo, threaded" / "something to make this weekend" / "it's almost done"

### Social media

**Instagram:** Process-first. Show hands threading, time-lapses, before/after reveals. Calm lifestyle staging works: a hot cup of tea or coffee, incense, a quiet space. Captions short and conversational. Use 3 to 5 relevant hashtags, not 30.

**TikTok:** Hook in the first 2 seconds. "Watch me turn this photo into string art" beats "Check out our product." Let the process sell itself.

### Packaging and inserts

Minimal text. Let the materials speak. A short welcome line, clear setup instructions, and a single invitation to share their finished piece. The unboxing should feel like opening a gift, not reading a manual.

### App copy (UI/UX)

Microcopy should be friendly and functional. Present tense. Guide without over-explaining. Celebrate milestones: "50% threaded, looking beautiful." Error messages should be helpful, not robotic: "That image is a little too small. Try one that's at least 1000px wide."

## 6. Product Language

Use these terms exactly as shown across all channels.

| Term | Usage |
| --- | --- |
| Kit | The complete product: loom, pins, thread, and app access. |
| Loom | The circular frame with 240 pins. Not "board," "frame," or "ring." |
| Pins | The 240 anchor points on the loom. Not "nails" or "pegs." |
| Pattern | The numbered threading sequence generated by the app. |
| Thread | Singular when referring to the material ("thread," not "threads"). Plural only for multiple color spools. |
| Piece | The finished string art. Not "product" or "output." |
| Make / Create / Thread | Preferred verbs. Not "build," "assemble," or "construct." |
| Upload | The action of adding a photo to the app. Keep it simple. |

## 7. Do's and Don'ts

| Do | Don't |
| --- | --- |
| Use "you" and "your" | Make "we" the subject of every sentence |
| Write short, clear sentences | Write long, complex paragraphs |
| Describe the feeling of making | Lead with technical features |
| Use active voice | Default to passive voice |
| Say "make" or "create" | Say "assemble" or "construct" |
| Use lowercase subject lines in email | USE ALL CAPS OR EXCESSIVE PUNCTUATION!!! |
| Let the process sell the product | Use urgency tactics or FOMO |
| Celebrate the maker, not the brand | Center the brand as the hero |
| Be honest about skill level ("no experience needed") | Overpromise or exaggerate results |

## 8. SEO and Keyword Strategy

Integrate keywords naturally. Never sacrifice readability for search. If a keyword doesn't fit the sentence, rewrite the sentence or skip the keyword.

**Primary keywords:** string art kit, personalized string art, photo to string art, custom string art, craft kit for adults, mindful crafting, DIY string art, string art from photo.

**Secondary / long-tail:** unique personalized gift, handmade wall art, meditative craft activity, creative gift for adults, photo art kit, custom wall hanging, relaxing hobby for adults, craft experience gift.

**Tone rule:** Keywords should feel invisible. If someone can tell a sentence was written for SEO, rewrite it. Copy reads naturally first and ranks second.

## 9. Formatting Rules

### Capitalization

Sentence case for headlines and CTAs ("Start your piece," not "Start Your Piece"). Title case only for the brand name: Threaditate. Product names lowercase: kit, loom, pattern.

### Punctuation

Use the Oxford comma. Em dashes with no spaces ("thread by thread—your photo appears"). Avoid exclamation marks in body copy; one per email maximum if needed. Ellipses are acceptable sparingly for pacing in marketing copy.

### Numbers

Spell out one through nine. Use numerals for 10 and above. Always use numerals for product specs: 240 pins, 20-inch loom. Use numerals for prices: $79.

### CTAs

Soft and inviting, never demanding. Preferred: "Start creating," "Explore kits," "See how it works," "Make yours." Avoid: "Buy now," "Shop now," "Don't wait," "Order today."
